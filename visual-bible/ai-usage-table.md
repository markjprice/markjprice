| OpenAI/NBER analysis of 1.5 million ChatGPT conversations            | About 70% of consumer usage was non-work. Practical guidance, information seeking, and writing accounted for about 77% of conversations. Asking for information or advice represented 49% of messages, compared with 40% asking ChatGPT to perform a task. Tutoring and teaching alone represented 10.2%.        | The current proposal overweights professional production and underweights learning, personal decisions, and everyday guidance.                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Pew Research Center, 5,119 US adults, February 2026                  | 49% used AI chatbots and 24% used them daily. Uses included information searching, 42%; work tasks among employed adults, 38%; entertainment, 25%; image or video creation, 24%; medical advice, 20%; diet and fitness, 20%; emotional support, 10%; companionship, 4%.                                          | Add learning and advice, image creation, and high-stakes safety. The book should not pretend users restrict ChatGPT to documents and office work. [Pew report](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)                                                             |
| Google/Ipsos, 21,000 adults in 21 countries                          | 66% reported using an AI application during the previous year. Among AI users, 74% used AI to learn something new or understand a complex subject. Among parents, 49% had used it to explore changing careers, earning more, or starting a business.                                                             | Learning should be one of the book’s promises. Book 4 should cover advancement in an existing career as prominently as freelancing. [Ipsos survey](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/google-ipsos-multi-country-ai-survey-2026), [Google’s learning analysis](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/our-life-with-ai-2025/) |
| Ofcom, 7,533 UK adults                                               | 54% used tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot. Ofcom observed uses including work, learning, recommendations, room planning, artwork, wedding speeches, relationship advice, and companionship. One in five UK online adults depended entirely on a smartphone for internet access.                         | Cover mobile and voice from the beginning. Include everyday and creative examples alongside professional ones. [Ofcom 2026 report](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/adults/adults-media-use-and-attitudes-2026/adults-media-use-and-attitudes-2026-report.pdf?v=415430)      |
| Gallup, 22,573 employed US adults, May 2026                          | 52% used AI at work, 30% frequently and 15% daily. Among AI users, writing and editing was used by 51%, research by 49%, problem-solving by 39%, email or communications by 29%, meetings by 19%, analytics by 18%, presentations by 17%, media generation by 17%, and automation by 16%.                        | Your work chapters are well chosen, but communications, meeting work, visual media, and process measurement deserve more explicit coverage. [Gallup’s July 2026 findings](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/712736/organizational-adoption-jumps-six-points.aspx)                                                                              |
| University of Melbourne/KPMG, 48,340 people in 47 countries          | Only 39% reported receiving any AI education or training. [Global trust and AI usage study](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/xx/pdf/2025/05/trust-attitudes-and-use-of-ai-global-report.pdf)                                                                                                                |
| Microsoft 2026 Work Trend Index, 20,000 knowledge workers who use AI | Respondents identified quality control and objective judgment as increasingly important.
